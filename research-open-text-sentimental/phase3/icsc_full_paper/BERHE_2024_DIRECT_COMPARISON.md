# Direct comparison with Singh et al. (2024)

**Prepared:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** prewriting comparison, not manuscript text. Primary source: [Singh et al., *Enriching Release Notes with Reddit Feedback*](https://okipublishing.com/book/index.php/okip/catalog/download/46/58/321?inline=1), especially pp. 1–5. Current-work sources are listed below. “Our work” means the present Phase 2/3 ICSC candidate, not every claim made in the earlier ICMLA submission.

## Verdict

**This is a different, narrower research problem inside the same research program.** It is a *conceptual follow-on* to the 2024 work because it investigates a missing post-level interpretation step in the same Reddit/software-update setting and uses the same ReleaseTrain platform. It is **not yet an empirical extension of the 2024 release-note system**, because it does not reuse the 2024 corpus, join release notes, measure breaking/critical impact, or show that the new filter changes the earlier prototype's decisions. Calling it simply “an extension of Singh et al.” would imply integration and improved downstream results that have not been tested.

**It does not overlap too much to be a distinct paper on the present evidence.** The research question, output label, evaluation unit, saved corpus dates, and experiments are materially different. The overlap is still substantial in domain, source infrastructure, motivation, some example concerns, and version/diagnostic observations. Novelty must rest on the explicit troubleshooting-purpose task and honest conditional evaluation, not on first use of Reddit for updates, first technical-issue discovery, first diagnostic-field analysis, or developer impact.

## What to say and what to resolve

1. Cite the 2024 study as the closest same-lab predecessor. Describe its release-note **impact/prioritization** goal and our **post-purpose** goal side by side. Do not treat it as an unrelated citation.
2. Report that the current corpus has 2025–2026 creation dates, making reuse of the 2024 study's post records implausible under the saved timestamps. The earlier paper's individual IDs are unavailable, so avoid claiming a verified ID comparison.
3. Ask Professor Berhe which collection and classification components of ReleaseTrain were shared, and whether the current API selection rules descend from the 2024 prototype. Document any reused code and its provenance.
4. If the authors want the paper framed as a **measured extension**, design an additional experiment that injects the troubleshooting filter into the earlier release-note workflow and measures what changes: retrieved-post precision, critical-issue coverage, or analyst decisions on a defined sample. Without that experiment, frame it as a related but separate task.
5. Narrow the diagnostic claim: the earlier study already notes version matching and technical symptoms. Our field counts provide a more systematic descriptive profile, but they are not evidence of actionability or issue resolution.

## Cautions in comparing numbers

- The earlier paper's “at least 100 posts per component” is a component-level count; unique IDs across components are not provided. Do not contrast “900” against “3,362” as if they were identically sampled benchmarks.
- The earlier paper's critical/breaking rate and our troubleshooting rate have different denominators, labels, and selection rules. They are not comparable prevalence estimates.
- Our 0.863 and 0.792 are classification metrics for different fitted versions and conditional test samples; the 2024 paper provides no corresponding TPS-versus-other F1.
