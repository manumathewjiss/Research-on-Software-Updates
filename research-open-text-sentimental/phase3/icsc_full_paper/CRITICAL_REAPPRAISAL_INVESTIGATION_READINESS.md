# Critical reappraisal: is investigation readiness a novel contribution?

**Date:** 2026-10-08. **Status:** prewriting research decision; no data, model, labels, or manuscript changed. This revises the recommendation in `SCIENTIFIC_STRATEGY_DECISION.md`: a binary investigation-readiness label is **not** itself a defensible novelty claim.

## Bottom-line decision

I would **not** make “investigation readiness” the named invention or sole new research question. Formal bug-report quality and missing-information research already ask what developers need and whether a report provides it. A simple present/absent field checklist, a binary readiness rating, and a suggested follow-up question have all been studied in closely related settings. The strongest remaining opportunity within this project is a **decision-conditioned evidence-gap study on informal, update-linked posts**: determine what the post claims, what evidence it actually supplies, what a reviewer can responsibly do next, and which *specific* clarification would change that decision. The new contribution would be the empirically tested transition from update-discussion purpose to a justified next action, not a renamed quality score.

That is a promising hypothesis and study design, **not a result already established by our repository**. There is no clearly stronger completed contribution hidden in the files; the current validated result is a conditional purpose classifier plus descriptive diagnostic-field counts.

The claim that the specific *combination* of update attribution, discussion purpose, missing decision-critical evidence, and next action has not been studied in exactly our corpus is a **bounded inference from this focused search**, not proof of worldwide firstness. Literature on app reviews, issue triage, and forum information remains relevant and must be cited.

## What our current evidence does and does not say

- `phase2/TASK_DEFINITION.md` records distinct software, update-link, and discussion-type decisions. Its `update_related=yes` rule includes a problem or change that **followed** an update; the user's later labeling instruction also includes posts where the author states, suspects, or asks about an update connection. These record an **author-reported connection**, not proven causality.
- `phase3/experiment_v1/RESULTS.md` and `phase3/fresh_test_20261007/RESULTS_FIXED_MODEL.md` test troubleshooting purpose **after human S2 scope selection**. Fresh macro-F1 is 0.792 on 344 S2 posts, including 67 other-update posts. This is not automatic discovery in a mixed Reddit stream.
- `phase3/diagnostic_results/RESULTS.md` contains 237 reconfirmed troubleshooting posts. Release details appear in 122, reproduction context in 108, error evidence in 40, and an attempted remedy in 118. Those are **observed-in-snapshot** counts, not developer-necessity judgments. The 100% specific-symptom rate partly follows from the codebook's troubleshooting definition.
- `phase3/fresh_test_20261007/saved_error_analysis/README.md` has 46 errors and provisional categories. The fresh posts do **not** have the diagnostic-field annotations, so an observed association in development cannot be used as an explanation of fresh errors.
- The master has stored comment context for all 542 Phase 1 records and 918 of 2,820 October API records, but `discussion_context` is limited/selected rather than a verified complete chronological thread. Thus “community fills the missing details over time” is a worthwhile future question, not a result this dataset can establish.

## Why a richer missing-information study can help—and when it does not

**Weak version:** label each post “ready/not ready”; count absent version, environment, steps, error, expected behavior, and remedy; call the sum a quality score. This duplicates established bug-report research and imposes inapplicable requirements. A version may be unnecessary for an evergreen service issue; an error log may not exist for a UI defect; a post can be useful as an emerging signal even if it cannot yet reproduce a bug.

**Stronger version:** for a defined engineering role, record a *next action* and its **minimal decision-critical gap**. Possible next actions: `investigate_now`, `ask_for_clarification`, `monitor_or_cluster`, `not_an_issue_for_this_role`, and `cannot_judge_from_snapshot`. If clarification is needed, identify at most one or two facts whose answer would change that action, not every absent field. Distinguish `not_observed`, `needed_for_this_decision`, `not_applicable`, and `not_evaluable_from_saved_text`. For update attribution, label what the author claims (`explicit_cause`, `suspected_cause`, `temporal_after_update`, `unclear`) and what *support* the text shows (e.g., named version, before/after comparison, rollback, repeated occurrence). Do **not** infer that the update truly caused the problem.

This is more than an annotation vocabulary only if we show reliable judgments, characterize informative cross-axis patterns, and evaluate whether the structured evidence changes a realistic triage choice. The fields alone are not a semantic-computing advance.

## Is a still stronger question hidden here?

Two alternatives were considered:

1. **Information accrual in Reddit conversations.** Potentially interesting because a sparse post may become informative after replies, and our original Phase 1 included comments. However, the current master preserves only partial/selected comments for many posts, and formal issue/forum research already studies later-added information and conversation context. Without complete chronological snapshots, this should **not** replace the main question.
2. **Update-causality attribution.** More domain-specific than generic quality: our label rule merges “after an update,” “the update caused it,” and “could the update be responsible?” A focused analysis of *claimed* attribution and supporting evidence could reveal an important risk in update-impact pipelines. It is promising, but actual causality cannot be established from Reddit text alone. It should be one axis in the next-action study, not a standalone causal-inference claim.

No substantially stronger, **already demonstrated** direction was found that stays within the present project. The most credible path is to use a single bounded study to test the decision-conditioned evidence-gap hypothesis.

**Not recommended as central work:** a generic “investigation-ready” binary classifier; an unweighted completeness score; automatic missing-field generation; a new transformer; sentiment; issue clustering; a partial-comment trajectory claim. They either duplicate literature, lack trustworthy ground truth, or move the paper away from its strongest evidence.

## Concrete study boundary

The single most valuable next study would use **existing posts**, not a new corpus. First pilot the next-action rubric on cases outside the final study set, then freeze it. Reviewers see only the saved title/body and a specified hypothetical role such as “maintainer of the affected product deciding the next triage step.” They do not see previous purpose labels, model scores, API pool, or each other's first pass. If they cannot identify the product from the post, that is an evidence gap, not a reason to invent a product. Report initial agreement and adjudication separately.

Primary analysis: among S2 posts, what next actions are supported, and how do these relate to the previously frozen troubleshooting-purpose labels? Secondary: which missing facts most often block a next action, and how much update-causation support is actually visible? Do not claim that an absent field causes poor outcomes or that the underlying update truly caused a failure. If testing an interface, preregister its task, randomization and metrics before viewing results. The existing classifier's fresh-test score stays separate and frozen.

**Decision:** I withdraw the earlier claim that *binary investigation readiness* is the single highest-impact improvement. I still recommend **one human-grounded study**, but its center should be *decision-critical evidence gaps and justified next actions in update-linked informal reports*. If the authors cannot resource that study, write the present conditional classification/diagnostic paper honestly; do not rename its field counts as a novel quality method.
