7. Main Contribution of the Paper. Please provide a brief summary detailing your
understanding of the paper's core contributions, goals, and primary ﬁndings.
When a new software update drops, Reddit gets ﬂooded with user posts. This feedback
usually falls into two buckets:
Technical Problem Solving (TPS): People reporting actual bugs, crashes, or asking
for/sharing workarounds. (This is gold for developers).
General Discontent (GDS): People just venting, complaining, or expressing fatigue without
any actionable technical details. (This is mostly noise for developers).

7. Main Contribution of the Paper. Please provide a brief summary detailing your
understanding of the paper's core contributions, goals, and primary ﬁndings.
The paper proposes a framework built to diﬀerentiate between technical user feedback,
such as bug reports, installation failures, feature confusion, and performance drops, and
messages containing general discontentment without technical details, denominated TPS
(Technical Problem Solving) and GDS (General Discontent), respectively. The work aims to
assist software development teams in ﬁltering the response to software updates for
actionable information they can base future updates on through several classical machine
learning techniques, like Naive Bayes and Logistic Regression, as well as NLP models
such as RoBERTa-base and VADER. Its main contributions are the creation of the gold
label dataset referring to the Reddit message threads, evaluation of classical ML,
transformer, and rule-based classiﬁers for TPS/GDS detection, and the introduction of
author-community sentiment trajectory
and divergence analysis. Primary ﬁndings include that classiﬁcation can be eﬀectively
learned using classical ML models and that TPS discussions show stronger author
community sentiment divergence than GDS discussions.

9. Weaknesses & Areas for Improvement. Detail the primary shortcomings of the
work. Please be speciﬁc about the raised issues. Please number major points to
help authors understand systematically.
1. The "technical_ﬁrst" policy deployed for the VADER baseline introduces a signiﬁcant
risk of class contamination, particularly given that the dataset is predominantly comprised
of General Discontentment (GDS). Keywords such as "error," "crash," and "ﬁx" are highly
polysemous in informal settings like Reddit (e.g., a user stating a UI change was a
"massive error" or demanding developers "ﬁx the app"). Because any comment
containing these keywords is automatically exempted from the GDS class, the baseline
likely suﬀered from a high false-positive rate for technical indicators. This keyword over-
saturation, combined with a very narrow neutral threshold (+/- 0.15), provides a clear
architectural explanation for VADER's low relative performance compared to the
supervised models. This is noted by the authors, but it does lead to a poor representation
of what one of sentiment analysis' main tools could actually do in such a context.
2. Labelling for the dataset used by the supervised classiﬁers is somewhat
underexplained. It would be interesting to know what criteria was used to label the
samples, considering it is implied actual software development teams were not contacted
for this work.

General Discontent (GDS) in Reddit software update discussions. The authors construct a

9. Weaknesses & Areas for Improvement. Detail the primary shortcomings of the
work. Please be speciﬁc about the raised issues. Please number major points to
help authors understand systematically.
1.The proposed TPS/GDS formulation appears closely related to existing software
discussion classiﬁcation literature. For example, prior work on "Mining Reddit as a New
Source for Software Requirements" already distinguishes categories such as bug reports,
feature requests, and other actionable user feedback. The paper does not suﬃciently
explain why these existing categories are inadequate or why TPS/GDS constitutes a novel
problem formulation rather than a relabeling or aggregation of existing classes. A more
thorough comparison with prior work is necessary to establish the paper's novelty.
2.The literature review is not suﬃciently comprehensive. Both the Introduction and
Related Work make broad novelty claims (e.g., "Most studies..." and "Very limited
work...") without adequately discussing the most closely related literature, particularly
work on software discussion classiﬁcation, software requirements mining from Reddit,
issue categorization, and bug report classiﬁcation. As a result, the novelty of the proposed
TPS/GDS taxonomy is not convincingly established.
3.The Related Work section is incomplete and does not suﬃciently cover the literature
most closely related to the proposed problem. While it reviews general topics such as
sentiment analysis, social media mining, and conversation analysis, it largely omits prior
work on software discussion classiﬁcation, user feedback mining, bug report
classiﬁcation, and Reddit/software forum analysis. As a result, the paper does not clearly
position its contribution relative to the existing software engineering literature.
4.The evaluation lacks comparisons with existing state-of-the-art or closely related
approaches. The experiments only compare diﬀerent classiﬁers within the proposed
framework (e.g., VADER, Naive Bayes, Logistic Regression, and RoBERTa), making it
diﬃcult to assess the practical advantages of the proposed TPS/GDS framework over
prior work. Including representative baselines from the related literature, or discussing
why such comparisons are not feasible, would strengthen the experimental evaluation.
