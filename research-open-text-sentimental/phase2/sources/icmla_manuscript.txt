Distinguishing Technical Problem Solving and
General Discontent in Reddit Software
Update Discussions
Manu Mathew Jiss and Solomon Berhe
Department of Computer Science, University of the Pacific, USA
Email: m_jiss@u.pacific.edu, sberhe@pacific.edu
Abstract—Reddit hosts large-scale software update discussions
where troubleshooting requests and general frustration often
appear together. This makes automatic interpretation difficult.
We present a framework that separates Technical Problem
Solving (TPS) from General Discontent (GDS) and jointly
analyzes author-community sentiment divergence in discussion
threads. This distinction is important because software update
discussions often contain both actionable troubleshooting reports
and general complaints, making it difficult for developer teams
to quickly identify discussions that require technical attention.
We construct a corrected TPSGDS-542 corpus (n= 542;
86 TPS, 456 GDS) and evaluate rule-based, classical machine
learning, and transformer models using stratified five-fold cross-
validation. Multinomial Naive Bayes provides the best minority-
class tradeoff, with Macro-F1= 0.659and TPS-F1= 0.463. We
further find that TPS threads show significantly higher author-
community sentiment divergence than GDS threads (0.371vs.
0.287,p= 0.009). These results suggest that TPS filtering can
help developer teams more efficiently identify troubleshooting
discussions within large volumes of update-related feedback,
while sentiment divergence provides additional insight into how
communities respond to technical issues.
Index Terms—Reddit, discourse classification, sentiment diver-
gence, software update discussions, Technical Problem Solving,
General Discontent, social media mining
I. INTRODUCTION
Social media has become one of the main places where
people discuss products, services, and technical problems. In
2025, global social media users reached 5.24 billion, which
is about 63.9% of the world population [1]. Reddit is one of
the largest discussion-based social platforms, with millions of
active users and thousands of topic-focused communities in
2025 [2]. Because users post quickly and interact in threads,
Reddit provides rich real-world text data for studying how
people react to software changes. Software update discussions
are especially valuable because they include direct user expe-
riences after releases. Users report bugs, installation failures,
feature confusion, and performance drops, while others share
workarounds and practical fixes. At the same time, many
posts express anger, fatigue, or disappointment without clear
technical details. This difference matters. A post that asks for
a fix should not be treated the same as a post that mainly
expresses frustration. In this paper, we call these two discourse

II. RELATEDWORK
Reddit has become an important source for studying large-
scale online discussions because of its topic-focused commu-
nities, threaded conversations, and publicly available user in-
teractions [5]. Prior studies have applied Reddit data to opinion
mining, topic discovery, software support, complaint analysis,
issue categorization, software requirements engineering, and
sentiment analysis, demonstrating its value for understanding
user-generated discussions across domains [3], [4], [6], [7],
[10], [12]–[14]. However, existing studies primarily focus on
software feedback categorization or general sentiment analysis
rather than distinguishing troubleshooting-oriented discussions
from general dissatisfaction or modeling author–community
interaction.
Conversation structure and temporal interactions provide
information beyond isolated text analysis. Reply chains cap-
ture stance and evidential relationships, while longitudinal
sentiment analysis reveals how emotions evolve throughout
online discussions [9], [11]. However, existing studies rarely
distinguish troubleshooting-oriented software discussions from
general user dissatisfaction or jointly analyze discourse type
with author–community emotional interaction. Our work ad-
dresses this gap through integrated TPS/GDS classification and
author–community sentiment divergence analysis.
III. METHODOLOGY
Figure 2 presents the overall experimental workflow, which
consists of three stages: (1) construction of a manually vali-
dated Reddit corpus with corrected gold labels, (2) TPS/GDS
discourse classification using rule-based and supervised learn-
ing methods, and (3) author–community sentiment trajectory
analysis for measuring discussion-level emotional divergence.
A. Reddit Corpus and Gold Labels
Software-update-related Reddit discussions were collected
using a open source software update monitoring platform that
provides access to Reddit posts through its API. Candidate
threads were retrieved from TPS-oriented and GDS-oriented
filtered pools. These platform-generated categories were used
only for candidate retrieval and not as ground-truth labels.
Similar Reddit-based data collection strategies have been
adopted in prior social media mining studies [7].
Each retrieved thread was manually reviewed and assigned
one of two discourse labels: Technical Problem Solving (TPS)
or General Discontent (GDS). The resulting TPSGDS-542
corpus contains 542 Reddit threads, comprising 86 TPS and
456 GDS discussions. Approximately 24% of the original
platform-assigned labels differed from the manually corrected
labels, demonstrating the limitations of heuristic filtering for
identifying troubleshooting-oriented discussions.
For each thread, we retained the post title, discussion
text, and metadata required for classification and sentiment
trajectory analysis.
B. Text Construction for Classification
For supervised classification, we constructed multiple text
representations from each Reddit thread. As shown in Fig. 2,
the primary representation,text_raw, combines the post
title, body, and up to ten highest-scored Reddit comments to
preserve discussion context [7], [15].
For feature ablations, we generated title-only and title+body
variants. Classical machine learning models used the cleaned
text representation, whereas V ADER and RoBERTa operated
on the original discussion text [4], [16].
C. TPS/GDS Classification Models
We evaluated four discourse classification approaches using
stratified five-fold cross-validation. A majority-class baseline
that always predicts GDS was included to quantify the effect of
class imbalance. Logistic Regression and Multinomial Naive
Bayes were trained using TF-IDF features with unigrams,
bigrams, sublinear term frequency scaling, and class-balanced
weights, following common practice in text classification [17].
RoBERTa-base was fine-tuned ontext_rawusing a max-
imum sequence length of 256, batch size of 8, learning
rate of2×10 −5, and two training epochs per fold [16].
For comparison, a rule-based V ADER baseline was applied
totext_rawusing atechnical_firstpolicy with
troubleshooting keywords such aserror,crash, andfix[4].
Discussions with strongly negative sentiment but no technical
indicators were mapped to GDS, using a neutral compound-
score threshold of±0.15.
D. Author–Community Trajectory Construction (C1 ′)
To analyze interaction dynamics beyond discourse classi-
fication, we constructed C1 ′ sentiment trajectories from the
complete chronological Reddit comment tree contained in
the raw API JSON data rather than the reduced TF-IDF
representation.

step-wise volatility, and early-versus-late sentiment shift for
divergence analysis and feature ablation experiments. When
no author reply was available, the sentiment score of the post
title was used as a fallback.
E. Sentiment Divergence and Evaluation
Using the C1 ′ author–community sentiment trajectories,
we quantified sentiment divergence as the absolute difference
between the mean author and community V ADER compound
sentiment scores [4]. Differences between TPS and GDS
discussions were evaluated using Welch’s t-test, the Mann–
Whitney U test, Cohen’sd, and bootstrap confidence inter-
vals. Robustness analyses were additionally performed on a
software-development subreddit subset. V ADER scores were
interpreted as relative indicators of author–community emo-
tional divergence rather than direct measures of affect [15].
Classification performance was evaluated using stratified
five-fold cross-validation on the 542-thread corpus with out-of-
fold metrics including accuracy, macro-F1, TPS-F1, precision,
recall, and PR-AUC [18]. Feature ablations compared title-
only, title + body, full-thread TF-IDF, trajectory-enhanced, and
subreddit-aware representations. Label reliability was assessed

IV. RESULTS
A. TPS/GDS Classification Benchmark
The rule-based V ADER baseline performs poorly overall
despite high TPS recall (0.896). Its low TPS precision (0.202)
indicates that many negative or technical-sounding posts are
incorrectly classified as troubleshooting discussions. This pat-
tern is consistent with the label-correction step, where heuristic
API labels were often noisy.
Among the learned models, Multinomial Naive Bayes
achieves the strongest balance between overall performance
and minority-class recovery, obtaining the highest Macro-F1
(0.659) and TPS-F1 (0.463). Logistic Regression achieves
the highest overall accuracy (0.845) but under-recovers TPS
discussions, with TPS recall dropping to 0.278. RoBERTa-
base does not outperform Naive Bayes in this setting, likely
because of the relatively small TPS class and the domain-
specific characteristics of Reddit language.

To assess annotation reliability, an independent annotator

Overall, the experimental results support three main find-
ings. First, TPS/GDS discourse classification can be learned
effectively using classical machine learning models. Sec-
ond, TPS discussions exhibit significantly stronger author–
community sentiment divergence than GDS discussions. Third,
Reddit post titles provide the strongest discriminative signal
for classification, while trajectory-based features and Tier-A
robustness analysis further strengthen the overall conclusions.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper presented a labeled benchmark and empiri-
cal study of Technical Problem Solving (TPS) and General
Discontent (GDS) in software-update Reddit discussions us-
ing a corrected corpus of 542 threads (86 TPS and 456
GDS). Learned models consistently outperformed the rule-
based V ADER baseline. Among all evaluated approaches,
Multinomial Naive Bayes with TF-IDF achieved the best
minority-class tradeoff, obtaining a Macro-F1 of 0.659, TPS-
F1 of 0.463, and TPS recall of 0.62. Fine-tuned RoBERTa-base
did not surpass this classical baseline at the current dataset
scale.
Beyond discourse classification, we analyzed author–
community sentiment divergence using C1 ′ V ADER trajec-
tories and found that TPS discussions exhibit significantly
greater divergence than GDS discussions (0.371 vs. 0.287;
Mann–Whitneyp= 0.009). Original posters in TPS discus-
sions were more frequently more negative than the surrounding
community (69.8% vs. 53.7%), and this separation remained
consistently higher throughout most stages of thread progres-
sion.
Feature ablation experiments further showed that Reddit
post titles provide the strongest discriminative signal for
TPS/GDS classification, while trajectory-based features im-
prove minority-class detection beyond full-thread text alone.
Tier-A software-subreddit robustness experiments and inde-
pendent human validation (91.2% agreement;κ= 0.688)
further support the reliability of the proposed framework.
Overall, the results demonstrate that troubleshooting-
oriented and discontent-oriented software-update discussions
can be distinguished effectively using discourse-aware NLP
while exhibiting distinct author–community interaction dy-
namics. Future work will expand the framework to additional
platforms and evaluate its practical usefulness with software
engineering teams.
