# Coauthor check of Codex's proposed labels

The two files `MANU_VERIFICATION.csv` and `BERHE_VERIFICATION.csv` contain the same 100 posts and Codex's proposed judgments. They are **review aids**. The proposals were generated by an AI assistant and are not human judgments.

Each coauthor should read the saved title, body, and available replies for **every row** in their own file. In `decision_accept_revise_uncertain`, enter:

- `accept` if you checked the post and agree with all three proposed fields;
- `revise` if you checked the post and disagree with at least one field, then fill all three `final_*` fields and explain the change in `reviewer_note`;
- `uncertain` if the saved text is insufficient or the rule is unclear, then explain why in `reviewer_note`.

Enter `reviewed_at` when you have made the judgment. Leave `final_*` blank for `accept` or `uncertain`. Reviewers should not copy a decision down the column without reading each post. They should work separately and avoid discussing individual answers until both files are complete.

The previous empty `pilot/reviewer_A_sheet.csv` and `pilot/reviewer_B_sheet.csv` remain the materials for a fully blinded study. These new files instead support a faster **AI-assisted coauthor verification**. Because both coauthors see Codex's proposal, their decisions are not independent blinded annotations. The paper may describe this workflow honestly after it occurs, but it must not call the proposals human labels or report agreement as if the two reviewers worked without seeing them.

If only one coauthor completes their file, report only that person's verification. If both complete their files, preserve both files, review disagreements together, and record the final resolution and who made it. Any count reported in the paper must use completed human decisions and explain how the posts were sampled.
