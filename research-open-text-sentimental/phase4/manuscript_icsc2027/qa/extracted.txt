Beyond Troubleshooting Detection: Diagnostic
Evidence and First-Pass Engineering Decisions in
Reddit Software Update Discussions
Manu Mathew Jiss and Solomon Berhe
School of Engineering and Computer Science
University of the Paciﬁc, Stockton, California, USA
m_jiss@u.paciﬁc.edu, sberhe@paciﬁc.edu
Abstract—Informal discussions after software updates mix
fault reports with advice, reactions, and general conversation.
A model that ﬁnds a technical post does not establish what an
engineer can do with it. We examine this gap by connecting
discussion-purpose classiﬁcation, observable diagnostic informa-
tion, and ﬁrst-pass engineering decisions in Reddit software
update discussions. The study combines a collection of 3,362 posts
with classiﬁer evaluation, diagnostic coding, and an exploratory
case study involving two independent software-engineering re-
viewers. Within human-established software-update scope, a title-
and-body TF-IDF logistic-regression classiﬁer achieved macro-F1
0.792 on a fresh test, outperforming majority and ﬁxed-keyword
baselines. This evaluation followed an earlier reserved test and
used the same selected conﬁguration ﬁtted on revised develop-
ment data. The smaller other-update class remained harder to
classify. Diagnostic coding showed that many troubleshooting
posts lacked visible reproduction context or error evidence. In
30 purposefully selected cases, reviewers sometimes cited similar
facts but chose different actions, including investigation, clari-
ﬁcation, and monitoring. Their explanations exposed differing
judgments about whether the available evidence justiﬁed acting
immediately or required further information. These observations
are exploratory and do not establish causal effects, population-
wide decision patterns, or downstream engineering beneﬁts.
Together, the ﬁndings distinguish identifying a post’s purpose
from interpreting its implications: purpose detection can organize
discussions, while ﬁrst-pass engineering decisions also reﬂect the
available diagnostic evidence and reviewers’ judgments about its
sufﬁciency.
Index Terms—software updates, Reddit, troubleshooting, diag-
nostic evidence, engineering triage
I. INTRODUCTION
Software updates generate feedback about failures, changed
behavior, and unmet expectations. Studies of problematic
application updates demonstrate the value of examining user
feedback after release [1]. However, collecting feedback is
only part of the problem. In interviews across product-
development companies, Tkalich et al. [2] found continuing
difﬁculties with interpreting feedback and using it in deci-
sions. Reddit and software forums provide additional places
where users describe problems outside formal issue-reporting
workﬂows [3], [4]. A post may report a crash, ask whether an
upgrade caused a failure, criticize a design change, or discuss
a forthcoming release. These messages share vocabulary while
serving different purposes.
A useful distinction is between recognizing troubleshooting
and interpreting what to do next. Consider two illustrative
reports: one says an application stopped working after an
update; another also describes the affected version, a working
comparison, and a failed remedy. Both can express trou-
bleshooting, while offering different grounds for investigation
or clariﬁcation. Even when two readers notice the same facts,
they may differ about whether to open a lead immediately,
ask a question, or monitor similar reports. Here, ﬁrst-pass
engineering decisions means this initial response to a saved
post, before reproducing the failure or establishing its cause.
Prior work already classiﬁes software feedback [3], ana-
lyzes diagnostic information in reports [5], and uses external
evidence to generate issue speciﬁcations [6]. Our contribution
concerns the relationship between these stages in informal
update discussion: what a purpose classiﬁer identiﬁes, what
evidence remains visible, and how reviewers explain an ini-
tial response to that evidence. This is an empirical ques-
tion about interpreting natural-language reports. A positive
troubleshooting label alone does not specify the sufﬁciency
of the information for a particular next action. A recent
mapping study likewise identiﬁes practitioner involvement and
evaluation beyond prediction accuracy as recurring gaps in
issue classiﬁcation [7].
We investigate three research questions. RQ1: Within posts
already judged to concern software and an update, how well
can title and body text distinguish troubleshooting from other
update discussion? RQ2: Which diagnostic facts are visible
in a reviewed sample of troubleshooting posts? RQ3: How
do two software-engineering reviewers use those facts when
selecting ﬁrst-pass engineering decisions for intact posts?
The study combines a reviewed candidate collection, a
ﬁxed text-classiﬁcation evaluation, diagnostic coding, and
an exploratory multiple-case study. Its contributions are: (1)
an explicit conditional purpose-classiﬁcation task with docu-
mented review and evaluation boundaries; (2) an evaluation on
previously unseen IDs, accompanied by a descriptive account
of observable diagnostic ﬁelds; and (3) a case-based analysis
of where reviewers’ ﬁrst actions converge or diverge despite
citing similar evidence. The semantic contribution is this ac-
count of how textual evidence supports different interpretations
and response choices. The classiﬁer supplies the ﬁrst stage of

the investigation; the case study does not evaluate a classiﬁer-
assisted workﬂow or establish a causal effect of diagnostic
ﬁelds.
II. RELATED WORK
A. Software feedback in Reddit and community forums
Iqbal et al. [3] examined Reddit as a requirements source
and classiﬁed posts as bug reports, feature-related feedback,
or irrelevant material. Tizard et al. [8] studied requirements
information in software forums. Wang et al. [4] later showed
that post-level context reveals feedback types and contextual
information that sentence-level analysis can miss. These stud-
ies establish both the value of community feedback and the
need to preserve its context. Our classiﬁer uses the saved title
and body, and the qualitative study keeps each post intact.
Its target is troubleshooting purpose conditional on an explicit
update connection, rather than general requirements relevance.
Recent Reddit research also extends beyond technical fault
detection. Zegers et al. [9] investigated software account-
ability concerns across software-focused communities, includ-
ing concerns with technical and nontechnical consequences.
This supports keeping dissatisfaction distinct from a blanket
nontechnical category. Singh et al. [10] studied how Reddit
feedback can enrich release notes for update-impact analysis
and prioritization. That separate project addresses a related
domain with a different unit and outcome: our study neither
joins release notes nor evaluates update-impact rankings. We
instead ask how individual update-linked posts express trou-
bleshooting and inform an initial response.
B. Diagnostic information and report quality
Bettenburg et al. [11] documented a mismatch between
information developers value and what reporters supply, in-
cluding reproduction steps and stack traces. Soltani et al.
[12] combined developer interviews, a survey, and repository
analysis to examine the signiﬁcance of bug-report elements.
Their ﬁndings caution against interpreting all ﬁelds as equally
important. Chaparro et al. [5] studied and detected missing
information in bug descriptions. More recently, Wang et al.
[13] analyzed initial and ﬁnal versions of web-application
bug reports and found that usage information and diagnostic
suggestions can emerge later in a report’s lifetime.
These studies provide the basis for treating the saved snap-
shot as a limited observation. A ﬁeld absent from our title/body
snapshot might appear later or in a comment. Our diagnostic
analysis reports ﬁeld visibility, not an overall quality score. It
also differs from ChatBR [14], which detects missing report
components and generates candidate additions using ChatGPT.
We do not generate missing incident facts. Instead, the human
study asks which observed facts and unresolved questions
reviewers cite when deciding whether to investigate, request
clariﬁcation, monitor, or take no update-focused action.
C. Connecting feedback with engineering work
Several studies evaluate steps beyond classiﬁcation. Tang
et al. [15] matched app reviews with historical bug reports
to guide reproduction and reported newly identiﬁed bugs to
development teams. Laiq et al. [16] studied industrial adoption
of invalid-report detection, identifying trust, tool integration,
and maintenance of accuracy as practical concerns. These
outcomes are stronger evidence of engineering utility than
our ofﬂine classiﬁer or exploratory reviewer decisions; we
therefore do not infer productivity or resolution beneﬁts from
our scores.
Two recent studies are especially close to the present
framing. Wang et al. [6] proposed an agent-governed retrieval
and generation framework that checks evidence sufﬁciency
and produces issue speciﬁcations for Firefox and VS Code.
K C and Hosseini [17] examined how Moodle forum feature
requests connect to issue-tracker records, combining trace-link
analysis with stakeholder interviews. Evidence sufﬁciency and
the movement from conversation to engineering work are thus
established research concerns. Our speciﬁc contribution is a
bounded study of ﬁrst-pass judgments on intact update-linked
posts, with paired explanations that expose both agreement and
contrasting action choices. It complements work on generated
speciﬁcations and cross-channel handoffs without claiming a
new general theory of triage.
D. Classiﬁcation evidence and study boundaries
Devine et al. [18] found that feedback classiﬁers can behave
differently on unseen datasets than on unseen applications
within a dataset. Laiq and Dobslaw’s mapping study [7]
further emphasizes generalizability and practitioner involve-
ment alongside accuracy. We consequently report a protected
unseen-ID evaluation separately from development compar-
isons and do not compare our F1 directly with scores from
other papers using different labels or samples. The small
human study addresses a separate interpretation question; it
does not validate the classiﬁer’s effect on reviewers. Across
these bodies of work, our distinguishing element is the joint
examination of purpose, observed diagnostic evidence, and
the reasoning behind ﬁrst-pass engineering decisions in the
software-update setting.
III. COLLECTION, REVIEW, AND STUDY POPULATION
A. Candidate collection and target
The research collection contains 3,362 unique records:
542 historical posts and 2,820 additional records from Re-
leaseTrain’s technical-problem-solving (tps) and general-
discussion (gds) candidate routes after ID overlap removal.
The routes supplied candidate pools, not reference labels.
These keyword-selected records do not constitute a random
sample of Reddit. Thirty records have inconsistent source
metadata: the investigators identify them as Reddit records
with a source-labeling error, while the archived URLs indicate
another platform. Historical ﬁts retain them; separate URL-
restricted development checks exclude them. We retain this
provenance limitation rather than silently rewriting the evalu-
ated populations.

Review proceeded in batches using explicit scope and pur-
pose rules. Provenance includes machine-proposed decisions

the entire corpus. Five combined records remained unre-
solved and were excluded from supervised experiments. A
later, deliberately stratiﬁed 200-post development check had
matching categorical answers across reported separate ﬁrst

correctness of the master labels or validate its full prove-

C. Reserved and fresh evaluations
An initial model-selection experiment used 2,084 eligible
development posts and 159 eligible cases from a 200-post re-
served review set. After development-label repairs, the selected
speciﬁcation and threshold were ﬁtted on a revised population
of 2,080 posts for a separate fresh evaluation. The reserved
and fresh reference decisions were jointly reviewed; they do
not supply independent annotator-agreement estimates.
For the fresh evaluation, an update-related API candidate
route returned 3,718 unique records. Removing 2,842 IDs
already in the master left 876; excluding 12 near-identical text
matches left 864. From sorted IDs, 500 were sampled without
replacement using seed 20261007, before consulting classiﬁer
outputs. Joint review retained 344 S2 cases. The original 500
spanned 66 communities, but only 45 were dated October 5,
2026 or later. Accordingly, this is an unseen-ID test, not a
wholly prospective temporal test. Its sampling frame remains
an API-ﬁltered candidate pool.
IV. PURPOSE CLASSIFICATION
A. Models and model selection
We compared a majority rule, a ﬁxed troubleshooting-
keyword rule, TF-IDF logistic regression, and a linear support-
vector machine (SVM), each using title-only or title-plus-body
input. The majority rule predicts troubleshooting. The keyword
rule uses predeﬁned malfunction, assistance, and recovery
cues, including crash, error, inability to start, workaround, and
rollback. Source route, community, timestamps, review ﬁelds,
and model-generated labels are not predictors. Comments are
excluded; an absent body contributes no text.

Preprocessing removes URLs, collapses whitespace, and
lowercases the saved text. TF-IDF uses word unigrams and bi-
grams, a minimum document frequency of two, at most 20,000
features, and sublinear term frequency. The vectorizer is ﬁtted
on each training fold only. Logistic regression uses balanced
class weights, the liblinear solver, and at most 2,000 iterations.
Both learned models were tested with C ∈{0.25, 1, 4} using
common stratiﬁed ﬁve-fold splits (seed 42). Selection used
pooled out-of-fold macro-F1, with troubleshooting F1 and
recall as tie-breakers.
The selected model was title-plus-body logistic regression
with C = 4. Its probability threshold, 0.5877, was chosen
on development predictions by comparing the default cutoff
with 37 score quantiles from 0.05 to 0.95. This development
selection is not an unbiased ﬁnal performance estimate. The
speciﬁcation and cutoff were ﬁxed before reserved testing and
retained for the revised ﬁt used on the fresh test.
B. Duplicate controls and sensitivity analyses
Before evaluation, exact matches to reserved records were
removed from development, and two further development
cases were excluded for title/body character-ﬁve-gram Jaccard
similarity of at least 0.92 to reserved cases. The fresh sample
applied the same near-duplicate threshold against the existing
collection. Subsequent grouped validation kept exact and high-
similarity development records together. The original selection
used stratiﬁed folds, so grouped validation is a robustness
check rather than a retrospective claim that selection had used
groups.

smaller class equal weight. We also report class precision, re-
call, F1, and confusion counts. Saved 95% percentile intervals
use 1,000 bootstrap resamples of evaluated posts. Paired model
comparisons resample the same posts for both models. These
intervals capture sampling variability conditional on the ﬁxed
labels and predictions; they do not incorporate uncertainty
about annotation, collection bias, or model selection.

Figure 2 shows that software product was usually visible

excluding them changes the descriptive ﬁeld rates only slightly.

They concern development predictions, not diagnostic annota-
tions of the fresh test. Joint coding provides no independent
ﬁeld-level reliability estimate.
VI. FIRST-PASS ENGINEERING DECISIONS
A. Design, selection, and reviewer task
We conducted an exploratory intact-post multiple-case study
with two external reviewers with software-engineering knowl-
edge. Thirty main cases were purposefully selected from re-
viewed development records, excluding reserved and fresh-test
IDs and duplicate matches. The cases span 18 communities,
at most three cases per community, and varied symptoms,
update situations, comparison evidence, remedies, and un-
certainty. Historical categories comprised 20 troubleshooting,
four dissatisfaction, and six other posts. These labels guided
diversity, not expected actions. Six separate pilot cases tested
the instructions; no confusion was reported and the guide was
retained. Pilot responses are excluded from main results.
Each reviewer received the same intact title/body snapshots
in a different order, with historical labels and selection reasons
hidden. The guide prohibited live lookup, comments, and
discussion before both ﬁrst passes were saved. It instructed
reviewers to focus on the author’s main request when multiple
changes appeared, judge the entire saved snapshot including
reported ﬁxes, and avoid equating emotional language with
lack of technical evidence. The coordinator reports that the
reviewers followed independent ﬁrst passes without outside
lookup.
For each post, reviewers chose one ﬁrst action: (A) open
an update-focused investigation lead, (B) ask a speciﬁc clar-
iﬁcation ﬁrst, (C) monitor or group similar reports, (D) take
no update-focused action, or cannot assess. A does not assert
that the update caused the problem. Reviewers identiﬁed the
most inﬂuential fact, explained their choice over the closest
alternative, stated uncertainty, proposed one question and
how its answer could affect the next action, and recorded
conﬁdence. No correct-action key was provided.
B. Analysis procedure
The two submitted workbooks were preserved unchanged
and joined by case ID. All 30 paired responses were ex-
amined for selected action, cited evidence, uncertainty, and
reasoning. A cross-case evidence matrix recorded converging
explanations, differing action thresholds, counterexamples, and
negative cases. We retained disagreements rather than adjudi-
cating them into one label. Counts describe these cases only;
we do not infer population frequencies or claim thematic
saturation. This is an investigator synthesis supported by
the response record, not a separately validated multi-coder
thematic analysis.

C. Convergence, disagreement, and negative cases
The reviewers chose the same ﬁrst action in 21 cases and
different actions in nine (Fig. 3). Both chose investigation
in ten cases, clariﬁcation in six, monitoring in one, and no
action in four. Reviewer A selected investigation 16 times
and Reviewer B 11 times; B selected monitoring ﬁve times
versus A’s one. These differences motivate examination of
explanations, not a claim that either reviewer was better
calibrated.
Concrete evidence can support a lead without resolv-
ing causality. Both reviewers chose investigation for a post
describing two affected applications and an unaffected com-
parison after an OS upgrade. They also agreed for a report of
several inaccessible home-automation pages, including back-
ups. Their reasons treated speciﬁcity and recovery impact as
grounds to investigate while leaving the cause open.
A missing distinction can matter more than the amount
of detail. Both reviewers requested clariﬁcation for a Chrome
crash case to distinguish normal from incognito behavior. A
ZFS case supplied commands and before/after space observa-
tions, yet both ﬁrst wanted to clarify the measured quantity. A
Kile case described a segmentation fault and a working clean
virtual machine, but both wanted a version or trace distinction.
Thus, technical detail alone did not dictate investigation.
A remedy can support different next actions. For
Firefox controls and Fedora autorotation reports containing
workarounds, one reviewer would investigate while the other
preferred grouping reports or monitoring wider scope. In a
VS Code terminal case with a workaround, both still chose
investigation. A single “remedy present” ﬁeld therefore did
not map consistently to one ﬁrst action in these cases.
Similar cited facts can support different thresholds for
action. In a screenshot case, both noticed the same visual
contrast, but one opened a lead while the other wanted
to distinguish image content from display behavior. For a
microphone indicator, one weighed recurrence and privacy
implications while the other requested identiﬁcation of the
active process. In a MySQL case, one reviewer asked whether
the failure persisted beyond the captured startup instant,
whereas the other treated reported inaccessibility as sufﬁcient
to investigate. These are interpretations of their explanations;
the study did not isolate the causes of disagreement.
Purpose labels and actions are distinct. Both reviewers
chose investigation for a historically other-labeled post re-
porting application breakage after a release-channel change.
Dissatisfaction-labeled posts with concrete UI changes yielded
investigation/monitoring or cannot-assess/monitoring pairs.
Conversely, both chose no update-focused action for selected
strategy, positive-feedback, preﬂight-checklist, and version-
lookup cases. These contrasts demonstrate conceptual distinc-
tions in this sample, not the frequency of mislabeled posts in
the corpus.
VII. DISCUSSION AND THREATS TO VALIDITY
A. Meaning for software-update triage
The three studies answer different questions. Purpose classi-
ﬁcation locates likely troubleshooting within reviewed update
scope. Diagnostic coding describes available facts. The case
study examines how readers interpret those facts relative to an
initial action. Their combination shows why a purpose label
or a count of visible ﬁelds should not be treated as an action
recommendation.
The practical implication is a possible organizing workﬂow:
surface a relevant report, preserve its evidence and uncertainty,
and let an engineer decide whether to investigate, clarify, or
monitor. A useful representation would retain the relationship
between an update, a symptom, a comparison, and a reported
remedy, rather than only counting ﬁeld names. This is an
implication for future design, not an evaluated system. The
present study observes neither issue resolution nor engineering
time saved.
B. Validity boundaries
Collection and generalization. Keyword/API selection,
source–class associations, the 30 source-metadata conﬂicts,
and restriction to reviewed scope limit representativeness.
Source-restricted development checks preserve the broad clas-
siﬁer conclusion but do not revalidate the historical tests on
a corrected corpus. Neither class proportions nor qualitative
action frequencies estimate Reddit-wide behavior. Fresh IDs
are not necessarily later events, and developers of the affected
products were not recruited as incident owners.
Labels and construct validity. Annotation includes assisted
and joint review. A selected independent check cannot estab-
lish corpus-wide reliability, and diagnostic reconﬁrmation ex-
posed scope discrepancies. A visible version, error, or remedy
is not a veriﬁed cause or a usefulness measure. Other update
combines heterogeneous discussion types. The classiﬁer does
not predict ﬁrst actions or independently determine update
scope.

Evaluation and conclusion validity. Development selec-
tion, subsequent audits, and different ﬁts require explicit
version boundaries. Bootstrap intervals condition on the saved
labels and predictions; they do not resolve correlated incidents
or annotation uncertainty. Duplicate controls reduce obvious
overlap but may miss paraphrased reports. The fresh minority
class is small. Grouped and representation comparisons remain
development-only. The absence of a reliable MiniLM gain
does not establish equivalence of all lexical and semantic
models.
Human-study validity. Two reviewers and 30 deliberately
varied cases support an exploratory account, not saturation,
population reliability, correct decisions, or causal inﬂuence
of evidence. Independence and qualiﬁcations are coordinator-
reported process facts. Investigator interpretation may favor
the proposed purpose/evidence/action distinction; contrary and
negative cases were retained to challenge it. Saved bodies can
include later ﬁxes, omit comments or inaccessible media, and
differ from the information available at ﬁrst publication. No
passage was experimentally removed and no real queue was
observed.
C. Research ethics and reproducibility
Reviewer ﬁles use study IDs, and the manuscript para-
phrases examples without usernames or direct post links. The
qualitative task used saved text and did not contact post
authors. Versioned manifests, source hashes, review guides,
analysis outputs, and model settings support internal traceabil-
ity. We make no claim here that raw text or reviewer responses
are publicly redistributable.
Author conﬁrmation required: Insert the name of the in-
stitutional oversight body, its actual determination, and the
reviewer-consent statement from the private study record be-
fore submission. Completion of these checks has been reported
by the study coordinator; exact wording has not yet been
supplied.
VIII. CONCLUSION
Within human-established software-update scope, title/body
TF-IDF logistic regression separated troubleshooting from
other discussion more effectively than majority and keyword
rules, reaching macro-F1 0.792 on a fresh unseen-ID sample.
Diagnostic coding showed that reproduction context and error
evidence were often not visible in saved troubleshooting text.
In 30 intact cases, two external reviewers sometimes selected
different ﬁrst actions while citing similar facts. Together, these
ﬁndings distinguish discussion purpose from the interpreta-
tion of diagnostic evidence: identifying troubleshooting can
organize feedback, while ﬁrst-pass engineering decisions also
reﬂect uncertainty and judgments about whether the evidence
is sufﬁcient. The study supports this bounded distinction, not
an automated or causally validated triage policy.
